# Nekbaar — Parenting, Wellbeing & Partner Content Strategy

## Product promise
Nekbaar helps caregivers manage household life and each child's development while supporting parents as people. Emotional wellbeing and practical parenting education are foundational product capabilities.

## Two distinct management spaces
### Household space
Owns shared household responsibilities: family calendar, chores, household shopping, meal planning, household budgets, bills, shared routines, family occasions, and shared memories.

### Individual child space
Each child has a separate profile and timeline for health, education, physical activity, emotional and social development, faith and values, sleep, nutrition, interests, hobbies, and milestones.

### Shared context without duplicated ownership
A family calendar can display a child's appointment, but the medical details stay in that child's record. A household shopping list can link to a child's supply need without copying the child's profile. The home dashboard can summarize both spaces without blending their data.

## Psychological and parenting experience
- Parent support: compassionate messages for mothers and fathers, reminders that caregiving does not require perfection, encouragement to ask for help, and practical ways to share responsibilities.
- Child emotional development: age-aware emotion naming, listening prompts, co-regulation activities, behavior observations, and referral guidance when a concern persists.
- Parenting education: short actionable guidance, scenario-based learning, family exercises, and age-appropriate activities.
- Parent notification center: always include supportive messages for the active parent, separately categorized from household reminders, child-specific alerts, and account/security notices.
- Tone: respectful, culturally aware, non-judgmental, inclusive of different family structures, and available in Arabic and English.
- Boundaries: no diagnosis, no unsupported treatment claims, no medication advice, no guilt-based messaging, and no replacement of qualified professional care.

## Partner ecosystem
Potential partner categories:
1. Child-rearing, early-development, and family education centers.
2. Licensed or appropriately credentialed psychological support and counseling providers.
3. Qualified child-development, pediatric, nutrition, and educational specialists within their professional scope.
4. Vetted parenting educators and educational content creators.
5. Nurseries, schools, sports academies, and child activity providers where relevant.

### Partner content formats
- Expert-reviewed articles and short videos.
- Live or recorded workshops and parent sessions.
- Guided family activities and age-specific learning paths.
- Clearly scoped appointment/referral listings.
- Q&A sessions with verified specialists.
- Co-created campaigns around parenting skills and family wellbeing.

### Trust and safety requirements
- Verify partner identity, credentials, scope of practice, and contact information before publishing professional claims.
- Require editorial review for health, psychological, developmental, and safety-related content.
- Clearly label sponsored placements and disclose commercial relationships.
- Moderate creator submissions and community discussions.
- Obtain explicit, specific family consent before any personal data is shared with a partner.
- Never expose a child's private profile, health history, mood log, or family notes to a partner by default.
- Keep professional guidance distinct from advertising, testimonials, and general creator opinions.
- Provide correction and takedown processes for inaccurate or harmful content.

## Content operating model
Every content item should identify:
- Audience: parent, household, or a specific child age range.
- Domain: household, health, education, physical activity, emotional wellbeing, faith/values, or daily living.
- Format: read, watch, listen, practice, record, or ask an expert.
- Intended action: what the parent can do after consuming it.
- Source and reviewer: author, credentials where relevant, evidence/source links, reviewer, and review date.
- Safety boundary: when the content is general education and when a professional should be consulted.

## Experience loop
Discover → Learn → Practice → Record → Reflect → Get support.

The goal is not to maximize notifications or screen time. It is to give parents a useful next step and help them understand patterns over time.


## Verified expert publishing network

The expert network is an editorial layer across the six product tracks, not a separate seventh track.

### Contributor lifecycle
1. Contributor submits identity, professional credentials, specialty, scope of practice, biography and evidence.
2. Editorial administration reviews identity and credentials before any professional verification claim is displayed.
3. Contributor profile remains accurately labelled as pending/unverified until approval; no placeholder experts, invented qualifications, fabricated ratings or implied endorsements.
4. Submitted material enters editorial review. Medical, psychological, developmental and safety material requires an appropriately qualified reviewer.
5. Approved content is tagged with its audience, age range, specialty, track, format, publication availability, sources, review date, safety note and related next steps.
6. Content can be corrected, unpublished or withdrawn, with reviewer and change history retained.

### Family discovery experience
- Families discover content by topic, specialty, child age and format.
- Every article, video, real audio item, visual guide, activity or Q&A response has one useful next action and links to related content.
- Expert profile pages may show approved contributions and a follow/favourite action only when those product interactions are implemented.
- No booking, direct consultation or clinical relationship is implied unless a real service and provider workflow exist.
- Sponsored content is visibly disclosed and kept distinct from independent professional guidance.

### Data contract
Each item should include a stable ID, title, summary, contributor ID, specialty, track, age band, audience, format, publication availability state, editorial status, reviewer ID, review date, source references, safety note, related item IDs and next action. This contract should be implemented against the existing architecture before introducing a new database model.

## Future feature release and communication boundary

- Do not frame upcoming features around commercial access or pricing.
- Do not publish pricing, trials, billing intervals, usage limits or payment-provider claims.
- Public teasers for features that are not ready use only a neutral “قريبًا” / “Coming soon” message.
- Do not request or collect a child's photo before the feature, privacy controls, guardian-consent flow and deletion controls are implemented and reviewed.
- Any future feature must pass product, editorial, privacy, child-safety and technical readiness checks before it is announced as available.
- Availability and release decisions must be communicated accurately, without implying that a feature is currently usable when it is not.
